# AGENTS.md
# Composition Study — Project Governance & Learning Protocol

> A systematic composition curriculum integrating Arnold Schoenberg's compositional pedagogy, classical Harmony & Counterpoint, repertoire analysis, and original composition.
>
> **Core principle: Learn by composing. Compose by understanding.**

---

## 1. Project Mission

This repository manages the user's long-term study of musical composition.

The objective is not merely to accumulate music theory knowledge or complete textbook exercises. It is to progressively develop the ability to independently conceive, notate, harmonize, develop, arrange, and complete original musical works.

The learning process integrates three sources:

1. **Arnold Schoenberg — Models for Beginners in Composition**
   - Provides the primary compositional progression.
   - Exercises should be followed systematically, but may be extended when pedagogically justified.
   - The emphasis is on generating musical material, developing motives, and constructing coherent musical forms.

2. **Harmony & Counterpoint (H&C)**
   - Provides the technical vocabulary and compositional tools.
   - Previously studied material must be actively incorporated into writing assignments.
   - Knowledge should gradually progress from recognition to independent application.

3. **The user's musical memory and repertoire**
   - Familiar classical compositions provide material for transcription, analysis, transformation, and inspiration.
   - The user should increasingly transform implicit listening experience into explicit compositional knowledge.
   - Original composition must remain the final objective.

### Long-term objective

Develop the ability to compose independently:

- Melodies with recognizable motives.
- Harmonically coherent musical phrases.
- Independent and expressive bass lines.
- Multiple interacting voices.
- Sentences, periods, and larger musical forms.
- Piano compositions, chamber music, and eventually orchestral works.

The user is particularly interested in the musical languages of Schubert, Beethoven, Mahler, Ravel, Debussy, and other major composers.

Do not restrict the curriculum to imitation of these composers.

The user is responsible for:

ChatGPT provides:

## 3. Source Hierarchy and Academic Integrity

The repository contains several kinds of knowledge. They must remain distinguishable.

### Primary sources

1. Schoenberg's original textbook and scanned musical examples.
2. The user's H&C lecture notes.
3. Original musical scores and authorized editions.
4. The user's own MuseScore compositions.

### Secondary sources

1. ChatGPT explanations and corrections.
2. Codex-generated music-theoretical analyses.
3. Repertoire analysis and external commentary.

### Rules

- Never present an AI interpretation as Schoenberg's original statement.
- Record the exact chapter, lesson, page, or exercise number whenever possible.
- Distinguish original textbook requirements from additional exercises designed by ChatGPT.
- If a theoretical interpretation is uncertain, explicitly mark it as provisional.
- When two sources disagree, record the disagreement rather than silently choosing one.
- Never modify the user's original score to make it conform to an AI interpretation.

**Copyright consideration:** Do not commit complete copyrighted textbook scans to a public repository without appropriate authorization. Store bibliographic information, exercise references, original notes, and permitted excerpts. Keep restricted scans in private or local storage.

## 4. Curriculum Architecture

The curriculum should progress through increasing levels of compositional independence.

Do not organize learning exclusively around the number of pages read.

### Stage I — Melody and Harmony

Primary source: Schoenberg, Chapter I, Sections A–C.

Relevant H&C lessons:

- L3: Minor Scales
- L4: Intervals
- L5: Triads
- L6: Figured Bass
- L7: Combining Triads and Figured Bass
- L8: Seventh Chords
- L9: Numbers in Music Theory
- L10: Non-Chord Tones
- L17: Harmonic Function
- L18: Harmonic Analysis

Skills:

- Constructing melodies from limited harmonic materials.
- Breaking chords into melodic patterns.
- Passing tones, neighbor tones, appoggiaturas, anticipations, escape tones, and double neighbors.
- Harmonizing existing melodies.
- Understanding chord inversions.
- Distinguishing harmonic roots from actual bass notes.
- Designing bass lines independently.

Expected outputs:

- Two-measure exercises.
- Four-measure phrases.
- Eight-measure original themes.

### Stage II — Motivic Development and Musical Form

Primary source: Schoenberg's sections on Sentences and other phrase structures.

Relevant H&C lessons:

- L11: Classical Cadences
- L12: Sentences and Periods

Skills:

- Basic Idea and Presentation.
- Repetition and varied repetition.
- Fragmentation.
- Sequence.
- Liquidation.
- Antecedent and Consequent.
- Half Cadence (HC).
- Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC).
- Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC).

Expected outputs:

- Eight-measure Sentence.
- Eight-measure Period.
- Sixteen-measure musical structures.

### Stage III — Voice Leading and Bass Line

Relevant H&C lessons:

- L14: Voice Leading in Keyboard Style
- L15: Resolving V7 to I
- L18: Harmonic Analysis
- L19: Using Inverted Dominants
- L22: Listening Below the Surface
- L23: Figured Bass and Non-Chord Tones
- L24: Cadential Six-Four

Skills:

- Root position and inversions.
- Contrary, similar, and parallel motion.
- Parallel fifths and octaves.
- Leading-tone resolution.
- Chordal seventh resolution.
- Bass passing notes.
- Pedal points.
- Cadential six-four.
- Harmonic rhythm.

Expected outputs:

- Independent bass lines.
- Two-part writing.
- Piano-style harmonic realization.
- More sophisticated phrase harmonization.

### Stage IV — Expanded Harmonic Vocabulary

Relevant H&C lessons:

- L16: Building Your Vocabulary
- L20: The Subdominant Function
- L21: Three Uses of the Submediant
- L25: Leading-Tone Seventh Chords
- L26: Applied Chords to V

Skills:

- Harmonic minor and melodic minor.
- Dominant seventh chords.
- Leading-tone seventh chords.
- Applied dominants.
- Applied leading-tone chords.
- Chromatic alterations.
- Secondary dominants.
- Harmonic reinterpretation.

Expected outputs:

- Chromatic phrases.
- Applied-dominant progressions.
- Modulating passages.
- More expressive harmonic writing.

### Stage V — Counterpoint and Texture

Skills:

- Two-part counterpoint.
- Independent melodic lines.
- Imitation and motivic exchange.
- Three-part and four-part writing.
- Piano accompaniment textures.
- Interaction between melody and bass.
- Harmonic and contrapuntal coordination.

Expected outputs:

- Two-part compositions.
- Three-part textures.
- Small chamber works.

### Stage VI — Larger Forms and Orchestration

Skills:

- Binary and ternary forms.
- Theme and variations.
- Larger thematic development.
- Instrumental ranges and timbres.
- Orchestration.
- Integration of harmony, counterpoint, melody, and form.

Expected outputs:

- Complete piano pieces.
- Chamber compositions.
- Orchestral arrangements.

The stages are not rigid prerequisites. Previously studied material should be revisited whenever a composition requires it.

## 6. Exercise Design Protocol

Every new assignment must contain the following sections.

### A. Learning objectives

Explicitly identify:

- The new compositional concept.
- The previously studied H&C concepts being reinforced.
- The expected musical skill to be developed.

### B. Musical constraints

Specify:

- Key.
- Time signature.
- Length.
- Harmonic framework.
- Melodic requirements.
- Bass-line requirements.
- Voice-leading requirements.
- Formal requirements.

Do not impose unnecessary restrictions.

### C. New concepts

Introduce at most one or two major new concepts per assignment.

Additional requirements should primarily reinforce previously studied material.

### D. Creative freedom

The user should be encouraged to:

- Extract motives from remembered repertoire.
- Change intervals and rhythm.
- Experiment with different harmonic interpretations.
- Create alternative bass lines.
- Explore multiple valid compositional solutions.

Do not force all exercises into the same harmonic vocabulary.

### E. Expected output

Specify the required MuseScore file and any supplementary analysis.

Exercises should gradually increase in scope.

Two-measure exercises are useful for technical experiments, but should not become the default unit of composition.

Use four-, eight-, sixteen-measure, or larger forms whenever the musical objective requires them.

## 7. Score File Management

MuseScore `.mscz` files are primary composition artifacts.

### Mandatory rules

1. Never overwrite the original submitted score.
2. Preserve every submitted version.
3. Store revisions separately.
4. Generate PDF, MusicXML, MIDI, and audio as derivatives when useful.
5. Keep score analysis linked to the exact score version.
6. Never claim that a revision was made unless the revised file exists.

### Suggested naming convention

`No006_original.mscz`

`No006_revision_01.mscz`

`No006_revision_02.mscz`

`No006_export.musicxml`

`No006_export.pdf`

`No006_review.md`

If the actual repository already uses another consistent naming convention, preserve it.

### Score inspection

When a MuseScore file is submitted:

1. Inspect the archive and its internal score representation.
2. Export to MusicXML when possible.
3. Verify key signature, time signature, tempo, measure count, and instruments.
4. Extract pitches, durations, rests, ties, slurs, and voices.
5. Identify chords and bass notes.
6. Compare the extracted score with any screenshots.
7. Record uncertainties caused by incomplete notation or ambiguous engraving.

Do not rely exclusively on visual image interpretation when structured score data is available.

## 8. Score Review Protocol

Every composition review must distinguish five dimensions.

### 8.1 Melody

Evaluate:

- Motivic identity.
- Repetition and variation.
- Intervallic structure.
- Rhythmic organization.
- Phrase direction.
- Climaxes and relaxation.
- Melodic coherence.

### 8.2 Harmony

Evaluate:

- Roman numeral identification.
- Harmonic function.
- Chord inversions.
- Chord-tone and non-chord-tone relationships.
- Harmonic rhythm.
- Cadential preparation.

### 8.3 Bass Line

Evaluate:

- Scale-degree movement.
- Root movement.
- Inversions.
- Stepwise motion and leaps.
- Pedal points.
- Harmonic support.
- Relationship with the upper voice.

### 8.4 Voice Leading

Evaluate:

- Parallel fifths and octaves.
- Contrary motion.
- Similar motion.
- Oblique motion.
- Common-tone retention.
- Leading-tone resolution.
- Chordal seventh resolution.

Distinguish traditional common-practice rules from intentional stylistic departures.

### 8.5 Form

Evaluate:

- Presentation.
- Repetition.
- Fragmentation.
- Sequence.
- Liquidation.
- Sentence and Period.
- Cadential structure.

### Feedback format

Every review should include:

1. Overall assessment.
2. Accurate transcription of the score.
3. Successful compositional decisions.
4. Technical errors and their exact locations.
5. Explanation of the relevant H&C concepts.
6. Suggested revisions.
7. Remaining learning gaps.
8. Updated mastery status.

Classify criticism into:

- **Critical:** violates an explicit exercise requirement or creates a structural problem.
- **Technical:** a theoretical or voice-leading issue.
- **Optional:** an aesthetic suggestion or alternative compositional choice.

Never confuse stylistic preference with objective technical error.

## 9. Notation Conventions

Use the following conventions consistently.

### Roman numerals: chord identity

Examples:

- I
- ii
- IV
- V7
- vi
- ii6
- Cad. 6/4

### Arabic numerals: scale degrees and bass line

Examples:

- 1–4–5–1
- 1–6–4–5
- 5–4–3–2–1

### Chord tones

Always provide actual pitches.

Example:

`ii6 = E♭–G♭–B♭, with G♭ in the bass.`

### Figured bass

Distinguish inversion figures from scale degrees and chord members.

Do not use Roman numerals and Arabic bass numbers interchangeably.

### Markdown mathematics

In Markdown files:

- Inline formulas: `$...$`
- Display formulas: `$$...$$`

In ordinary ChatGPT explanations, use `\(...\)` and `\[...\]`.

## 14. Educational Philosophy

This project should not become a system for mechanically satisfying musical rules.

Its purpose is to develop compositional independence.

The user should gradually move through the following progression:

**Listening → Remembering → Transcribing → Analyzing → Transforming → Composing → Revising.**

Schoenberg provides a systematic path from simple musical materials to larger structures.

Harmony & Counterpoint provides the technical means to understand and control those structures.

Repertoire provides a vast reservoir of musical experience.

AI provides individualized explanation, technical inspection, feedback, and project continuity.

However, the final musical decisions belong to the composer.

The goal is not to produce compositions that merely satisfy an AI evaluator.

The goal is to develop a person who can independently understand why a musical passage works, identify why another passage fails, and create something new.

> **The ultimate measure of progress is not how many theoretical concepts the student has learned, but how much independent compositional control the student has acquired.**
